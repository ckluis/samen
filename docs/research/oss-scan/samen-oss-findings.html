Executive Summary & Scoreboard

This report triages 127 self-hosted Elixir/BEAM repositories (the awesome-selfhosted-elixir list) against samen's own architecture — its ADRs, its chokepoint invariants, and the open items in its gap register. Ten repositories cleared the bar for high relevance: projects mature or novel enough to change a concrete decision, either by filling a named samen gap outright or by supplying a battle-tested pattern worth reimplementing. This section owns all ten outright — their full write-ups live here, not scattered across the thematic deep-dives that follow, which are built entirely from the 50 medium repositories instead.

Three lessons repeat often enough across these ten to state once rather than re-litigate in every card. First, license risk clusters hard: Plausible Analytics, Lightning, Keila, and Health are copyleft (AGPL-3.0 or LGPL/GPL-3.0) and cannot be vendored into MIT-licensed samen under any circumstance — every recommendation attributed to those four is pattern-only, independently reimplemented, never transcribed from source. Second, the three agent-framework entrants — LangChain for Elixir, Sagents, and Loomkin — are each rejected as a dependency for the same structural reason samen already rejected ash_ai and Jido (ADR-037): none of them route prompt assembly through a masking chokepoint, and pulling any of them into samen would put vendor/HTTP deps or raw-string prompt handling inside samen_core, violating INV-4. Their value is real but lives entirely in patterns worth mining — trajectory-based regression testing, HITL decision UX, decision-graph memory — never in the library itself. Third, four of the ten map to a specific, already-named line in samen's own gap register rather than a vague good idea: AetherS3 fills the open Storage.S3 skeleton, Health fills G11's still-open public status page, Osmotic fills G13's stubbed usage rating, and Boruta Server fills the explicitly deferred OIDC-provider capability. Those four are the shortest path from "read this" to "close a line in the roadmap."

The ten adoption candidates

Each card below names the project and its license, the concrete thing samen should take from it, why that fits samen's architecture rather than generic best practice, and an effort badge for the primary recommendation (a project's finding file often lists several picks at different sizes — the badge here reflects the single highest-value one).

LangChain for Elixir high

Apache-2.0. The de-facto Elixir LLM/agent framework (not a Python port): an LLMChain agent loop, tool/function calling, twelve-plus provider support with fallback chains and prompt caching, and its own trajectory-testing and GenAI-semconv telemetry.

Adopt: trajectory-based agent regression testing — capture an ADR-047 run's tool-call sequence into a comparable structure with strict/wildcard/unordered/superset matchers, ExUnit assertion macros, and golden files — plus its actionable tool-argument error feedback and GenAI OTel semantic-convention spans on the AI chokepoint. M

Why it fits: this is samen's own "every guarantee ships with a proof" religion applied to agent behavior, complementing samen.verify.agent_coverage with behavioral regression pins that are themselves sabotage-able.

Sagents high

Apache-2.0 (Hex). Built on top of LangChain for interactive Phoenix LiveView agents: OTP-supervised per-agent servers, HITL approve/edit/reject decisions with per-tool-call granularity, a rich PubSub streaming-event taxonomy, and a dual-view transcript model.

Adopt: the dual-view transcript split — a compactable LLM-facing AgentState blob versus masked-token-only DisplayMessages — and the LiveView streaming event vocabulary for when ADR-047's deferred streaming lands; the :edit HITL decision type also enriches the E3 approvals engine beyond plain approve/reject. M

Why it fits: it's the closest existing analogue to samen's own agent loop, proven in production LiveView apps, and its patterns transfer without importing LangChain or its direct-execution-on-approval model (which would regress samen's "AI writes do not exist" invariant).

Plausible Analytics high

AGPL-3.0, patterns only. Mature (28.7k stars), privacy-first Elixir/Phoenix web analytics: no cookies, no persistent identifiers, PostgreSQL for accounts plus ClickHouse for the event store.

Adopt: rotating daily-salt SipHash visitor identity (structurally unlinkable across days, nothing PII-shaped ever at rest) for Samen.Scopes.Analytics, its closed drop-reason taxonomy at the ingestion chokepoint, and its streamed S3 zip-export machinery for the open DSAR self-serve gap (G19). M

Why it fits: "token-blind by construction" is exactly samen's own masking philosophy, applied here to analytics identity instead of vaulted PII — a natural sibling to the token-blind aggregate plane.

Lightning (OpenFn) high

LGPL-3.0/GPL-3.0, patterns only. A production workflow-automation platform (DAG jobs/triggers/edges) used by governments and NGOs in 40+ countries, on Elixir/Phoenix/Ecto/Oban — the closest thing to samen's own Automation engine running at real scale in the wild.

Adopt: snapshot-per-run workflow versioning (editing or deleting an action never corrupts an in-flight or historical run) and the WorkOrder⊃Run(s) retry model, plus libcluster_postgres for config-free multi-node clustering and its streaming-zip DSAR export shape. M

Why it fits: it validates and sharpens ADR-039's Action → Compile → Reactor design with an operator-grade "edit freely, history stays true" contract that composes cleanly with the already-adopted ash_paper_trail versioning.

Keila high

AGPL-3.0, patterns only. A self-hostable newsletter/campaign platform (2.2k stars) on Phoenix/LiveView/Oban with pluggable ESP adapters, a Mongo-style contact segment DSL, and MJML+Liquid templating.

Adopt: multi-scale per-sender token-bucket rate limiting — ETS buckets at second/minute/hour scales, persisted across restarts, Oban-snooze on throttle — and bounce/complaint-driven suppression semantics for the delivery chokepoint, both currently absent from samen. M

Why it fits: samen ships samen_postmark/samen_ses/samen_resend send adapters but no evidenced rate-limit or suppression-list logic; without it, any vertical emailing tenants' end-customers burns sender reputation with no chokepoint-level defense.

AetherS3 high

MIT. An actively developed, self-hostable, BEAM-native, distributed S3-compatible object store with an AP data plane (rendezvous hashing, CubDB) and a CP control plane (Khepri/Raft), plus an unusually candid "status and limitations" README.

Adopt: run it as the local/CI target for samen's own Storage.S3 skeleton — currently an explicit unimplemented operator-TODO in samen_core/lib/samen/files/storage/s3.ex — instead of mocking S3 or requiring live AWS credentials; its dual /ready vs /ready/cp readiness split is also a sharper template than samen's current /readyz. S

Why it fits: MIT license makes it directly runnable, not just readable, closing the fixture-availability half of a named gap in a docker-compose command.

Boruta Server high

Apache-2.0. An OpenID-Foundation-certified OAuth2/OIDC authorization-server umbrella (five sibling apps) with PKCE, DPoP, PAR, WebAuthn, and LDAP federation, developed daily.

Adopt: its core boruta library as an architecture reference — and a real candidate to vendor as a new samen_oauth adapter package, path-dep on samen_core only, matching the existing samen_stripe/samen_anthropic convention — for samen's explicitly deferred "become an OIDC provider" gap, plus wax_ for passkey/WebAuthn support on the existing identity spine. L

Why it fits: samen's ADR-035 identity spine only consumes OIDC today (via assent, logging users into samen); nothing lets a tenant's own integrations authenticate against samen, and standards-certified code beats hand-rolling PKCE/DPoP/introspection correctness from scratch.

Health high

AGPL-3.0, patterns only. A deliberately non-Phoenix/non-Ecto (Bandit + Plug + raw Postgrex) self-hosted uptime monitor with push mode (dead-man's-switch heartbeats, structurally similar to samen's fleet heartbeat) and pull mode (active HTTP/DNS/TCP/SMTP probing).

Adopt: the missing feature itself — a masked, publicly-viewable status page — is the exact open item under G11 ("/readyz shipped, public status page still open"); its pull-mode active probing also closes a real blind spot, since an app whose heartbeat job is wedged reads as healthy in samen's push-only fleet model today. M

Why it fits: samen already has LiveView and two-plane masking, so it needs Health's feature, not its SSE/Datastar transport; AGPL means reimplement, never copy.

Osmotic high

BSD-2-Clause. An early-stage but unusually well-documented self-hosted Elixir/Phoenix AI gateway: issues API keys, enforces spend/token quotas, scans outbound prompts for leaked secrets, meters cost per request.

Adopt: its idempotent, insert-only usage ledger (telemetry → ETS-buffered writer with a UUID idempotency key → immutable usage_records fact table with on_conflict: :nothing → live tallies rebuilt from source at rollover) plus an effective-dated, never-updated pricing table — close to a direct blueprint for the open G13 gap, where Stripe sync is presently a stub and usage rating/proration is operator-TODO. M

Why it fits: architecturally consistent with samen's own effective-dated rich types (ADR-036) and hash-chained-audit precedent for immutability; permissive license makes even direct code study viable, though 0-star/7-commit maturity argues for pattern-adoption over dependency.

Loomkin high

MIT. A persistent multi-agent development workspace (~179 stars, 800+ commits) on Elixir/Phoenix/LiveView, built on Jido + req_llm, with a Postgres-backed decision-graph memory and full-fidelity "keeper" context offload instead of lossy summarization.

Adopt: the decision graph as a new samen Ash resource family — goal/decision/option/action/outcome/observation/revisit nodes, typed edges including rejected/supersedes, confidence scores that cascade uncertainty downstream — written by the agent loop and injected into prompt assembly through the existing masking chokepoint; also its keeper-style full-fidelity context offload and diff-in-approval-modal UI. M

Why it fits: ADR-047's agent loop has transcripts and retention but no structured, queryable reasoning memory; the graph would stay crypto-shreddable (tokens only, per ADR-046) and give the operator plane a genuinely new capability.

Why the agent frameworks get mined, not merged

LangChain, Sagents, and Loomkin all assemble prompts and marshal tool results themselves, outside samen's EG2 governed-egress boundary — the same objection that already sank ash_ai and Jido (ADR-037). Samen's Samen.AI chokepoint mints a %Samen.AI.MaskedPayload{} and every provider adapter refuses a raw string with a FunctionClauseError; none of the three external frameworks have an equivalent concept, so adopting any of them as a dependency would either pull vendor/HTTP deps into samen_core (violating INV-4) or reopen a raw-prompt path the masking chokepoint exists specifically to close. The diagram below contrasts the two shapes — external library on top, samen's own chokepoint on the bottom — which is why the verdict on all three is identical: real design value, zero dependency value.

Net read for a product leader skimming only this section: four items (AetherS3, Health, Osmotic, Boruta Server) close named gaps and are worth scheduling directly; three items (Plausible, Lightning, Keila) hand over production-proven delivery/analytics/automation patterns behind a copyleft wall that only permits reimplementation; three items (LangChain, Sagents, Loomkin) are agent-framework pattern quarries whose libraries samen has already, correctly, decided not to depend on. Effort skews toward M across the board — one S (AetherS3, a docker-compose fixture) and one L (Boruta Server, a new adapter package plus real OAuth flows) bracket the set.

Deep Dive: Agent Frameworks & Orchestration Libraries

Eight self-hosted Elixir agent frameworks cleared the bar for medium relevance, and every one of them lands the same verdict: not a dependency, but a pattern quarry. Samen already BUILT its own agent loop (ADR-047, accepted 2026-08-17, zero new dependencies) after evaluating and rejecting ash_ai and Jido (ADR-037) for the same structural reason — none of them route prompt assembly through a masking chokepoint. All eight projects here fail that identical test: their LLM adapters accept raw strings rather than a chokepoint-minted %MaskedPayload{}, most pull ReqLLM or an equivalent multi-provider client (and therefore vendor/HTTP deps) toward what would become samen_core, violating INV-4, and two (Condukt, Cantrip's optional container runner) lean on Rust NIFs samen deliberately avoids. None of that makes them uninteresting — three ship real production engineering (BeamWeaver, Cantrip, Lemon), one is from a serious shop (Legion, Software Mansion Labs), and collectively they converge on the same handful of mechanisms samen's own gap register is missing. The value is entirely in mining those mechanisms, not in the libraries themselves.

Two lessons taught by six projects each

Rather than repeat the same mechanism eight times, the two patterns that recur most are worth diagramming once and attributing broadly. Streaming backpressure is the biggest cluster: Alloy's versioned event envelope, BeamWeaver's typed %Stream.Envelope{}, Cantrip's cast_stream, Lemon's bounded-queue EventStream, and Nous's pull-based Hackney [{:async, :once}] mode are all working answers to the one feature ADR-047 explicitly deferred. They agree on shape — normalize provider chunks into a typed, versioned event, bound the queue so a fast model can't overwhelm a slow LiveView consumer, and never let a raw provider chunk reach a subscriber — but none of them solve the part that actually matters for samen: re-scrubbing through the masking chokepoint per delta, not once per turn. That is samen's own work to do; these six are proof the surrounding plumbing is a solved, cheap, zero-new-dependency problem.

The second cluster is checkpoint-backed pause/resume for approval-gated runs, taught most completely by BeamWeaver and ElGraph (Legion's HumanTool and Lemon's steering queues are lighter variants of the same idea). Samen's rule is "AI writes do not exist" — every side effect becomes a draft routed through the E3 approvals engine — but today a multi-step agent run that hits an approval mid-flight has no first-class way to suspend and resume; it terminates into a draft and the operator restarts the surrounding context by hand. BeamWeaver and ElGraph both solve this the same way: checkpoint the run state keyed by a stable thread/run id after every step, let an interrupt persist durably (the OS process can crash during the wait without losing state), and have a plain resume/3 call continue from the exact boundary once the human decides — with already-completed steps and memoized tool calls never re-run, so approvals that take days don't re-bill LLM calls on resume.

Two smaller clusters are worth naming once rather than per-card: bounded-retry/fallback middleware at the chokepoint (BeamWeaver's ModelRetry/ModelFallback/ToolCallLimit, Alloy's block/edit hooks, Nous's transport-vs-application error taxonomy) all agree that retries and provider fallback belong inside the AI kernel's chokepoint, never in an adapter, and that only transport-layer failures — never validation or max-iteration errors — should trigger fallback; that discrimination already matches samen's :not_configured/:not_implemented fail-honest adapter contracts. And sandboxed code execution for LLM-authored compute (Cantrip's port-isolated Dune VM, Legion's Lua VM, Condukt's in-memory→microVM→K8s-pod ladder) all agree the safe move on the BEAM is a language that structurally cannot reach the host process — no NIF, no AST denylist to maintain by hand — which is why, of the three, only Legion's pure-Elixir lua package matches samen's existing no-NIF posture (the same reason it chose simple_sat over a SAT-solver NIF).

The eight, in detail

Alloy medium

MIT, ~9,000 LOC, 81★/118 commits, actively maintained. The most minimal entrant — "the agent loop and nothing else" — OTP-native, dependency-light, six provider adapters, a single Middleware.call/2 hook over eight lifecycle points.

Adopt: its until_tool pattern — the loop terminates only when the model calls a named tool, making the final output an API-schema-validated structure instead of a prompted format — as the terminal shape for samen's draft/proposal hand-off to E3. Its {:block, reason} / {:edit, modified_call} / {:halt, reason} contract at :before_tool_call (edited calls can't change id/name) is also the cleanest single-seam design seen across all eight for formalizing EG2's per-turn re-scrub as an ordered hook chain. S

Why it fits: samen's "AI writes do not exist" posture means every agent outcome already is a draft handed to approvals — a terminal proposal-tool with a validated schema removes parse-failure red paths entirely, and it composes with zero behavior change to the already-BUILT loop.

BeamWeaver medium

Apache-2.0, v0.1.18, 46★, pushed within the last week. The most complete LangChain+LangGraph+Deep-Agents port in Elixir: module-DSL agents, graph workflows with reducers/subgraphs, an extensive middleware-as-data library, and a RAG pipeline with incremental indexing.

Adopt: checkpoint-backed pause/resume for approval-gated runs (see diagram above) is the headline item, alongside its record-manager pattern — tracking indexed-document identity/hashes so re-indexing only touches changed content — as the missing hygiene layer for keeping tenant knowledge bases cheap to re-embed under G22. M

Why it fits: young (created 2026-06) and single-vendor, but the checkpoint-at-tool-boundary shape maps directly onto samen's Oban-backed loop, and both picks are pure Postgres/pgvector work with no new infrastructure.

Cantrip medium

MIT, ~122★/298 commits, actively developed. A multi-agent runtime whose governance vocabulary — gates (allowlisted boundary tools), wards (declarative runtime constraints), a forkable "loom" of turns — rhymes with samen's chokepoint philosophy more than any other entrant, making it a more interesting second data point than Jido.

Adopt: the port-isolated Dune sandbox — LLM-authored Elixir code evaluated in a child BEAM with no host-BEAM authority, injected proxy gates, a length-prefixed term protocol, and kill-on-timeout — as the reference architecture if samen's automation engine ever grows a "compute over already-masked results" step. Dune itself is a small, vendor-free hex dep that could live in an adapter package. L

Why it fits: it is the one code-execution shape structurally compatible with "AI writes do not exist," since sandboxed code and its inputs/outputs are still EG2-governed egress; effort is L and only worth scheduling once a compute step is actually on the roadmap.

Condukt medium

MIT (library) / MPL-2.0 (web server), ~37★/177 commits, published on Hex with unusually thorough docs. Ships as a Rust terminal binary, an Elixir library, and a WASM browser package; frames its architecture as "the loop is Condukt's, the reach is the host's" — tools, credentials, and network policy stay outside the session.

Adopt: surface-scoped tool registries — each agent surface (MCP server, operator plane, tenant plane, CI eval lane) owns its own tool registry so a tool registered for one surface structurally cannot be invoked from another — plus a secrets-redaction lane treated as a taxonomy distinct from PII masking, since operator/app secrets (API keys, connection strings) aren't pii_*-classed today. S

Why it fits: Condukt independently arrived at "host-owned tools, loop-owned history, credentials outside the session" — external confirmation that ADR-047's shape is the right one — and both picks are contract-plus-verifier extensions over existing machinery, not new subsystems.

ElGraph medium

MIT, ~v0.3, 2★/0 forks, single author but ~157 commits across five milestones. Adopt: its Ctx.interrupt/2 + ElGraph.resume(thread_id: id, resume: answer) mechanic is the cleanest worked example of the checkpoint/resume pattern in the synthesis diagram above, and its ElTrace timeline is a ready information-architecture reference for a masked-by-default agent-run debugging surface in the operator plane. M

Why it fits: 2 stars and a single author is real abandonment risk for a dependency, but the mechanic itself is proven enough to copy the idea rather than the code — and a fork-from-any-checkpoint primitive, cheap once resume exists, is a natural fit for samen's red-team eval tier.

Legion medium

MIT, v0.4.0, ~103★/41 commits — young, but from Software Mansion, a serious shop. Agents write Lua (or optionally Elixir) code that executes in a sandbox rather than doing classic tool-call round trips; tool functions are the only bridge out.

Adopt: diff Legion's Elixir-sandbox AST denylist (defmodule, import, spawn, send, apply) against samen's existing raw-spawn AST lock as a cheap adversarial audit that may surface misses like apply/3 indirection — worth doing regardless of any code-execution roadmap. Deep Dive: Software Factories & Multi-Agent Dev Platforms

Eight self-hosted Elixir projects here are a different species from the agent-framework libraries in the previous section: none of them are runtime dependencies a product embeds. They are development-process tools — control planes that sit outside the product, delegating coding tasks to Claude Code, Codex, or similar CLI agents against a real repo, gating the result behind CI and a human merge. That makes them irrelevant to samen's product kernel (no PII vaulting, no multi-tenancy, no billing surface among any of them) but directly on-topic for two things samen already owns and is actively hardening: its own _orch/ autonomous burn-down loop, and the in-product durable agent loop (ADR-047, BUILT, accepted 2026-08-17). All eight land the same verdict as the agent-framework set — pattern quarry, not dependency — for an added reason beyond the masking-chokepoint test: five of the eight ship under licenses samen's MIT posture can't vendor from at all (GPL-2.0, AGPL-3.0, MPL-2.0, FSL-1.1-MIT), so even the three that are MIT (Pixir, Shep) are being read here for design, not code.

Cluster 1 — the tracker-driven orchestrator shape, for samen's own _orch/ loop

Four of the eight independently converge on the identical service shape: a supervised process polls a source of truth (a tracker, a backlog file), claims exactly one item under a single-authority lock, prepares an isolated workspace, runs the coding agent inside it, verifies the result, and reconciles on restart by re-deriving state from the source rather than trusting in-memory history. Symphony's SPEC.md formalizes this most completely (claim state machine Unclaimed → Claimed → Running → RetryQueued → Released, restart recovery with no database — re-poll the tracker, reuse persisted workspaces); Shep implements a scrappier version of the same idea with GitHub labels literally as the state machine ("tracker-as-database": restart and re-learn the world from label positions, no Postgres/Redis/state file); Svärm wraps it in a GenServer poll over pluggable Tracker/Runner/Provider behaviours; ControlKeel runs a lighter-weight variant purely for verification (mine failures → draft eval candidates → human-gate promotion → auto-archive on results). This is the single most load-bearing finding in the section, because it maps onto a named samen pain point almost exactly: the _orch/ loop today is session-driven and manually cranked (backlog.yaml + per-task handoffs run inside a human-launched Claude Code session), with documented failure modes — session-limit hits stranding an agent mid-task, serialized fan-outs, phase-gate tasks deadlocking when backgrounded instead of run foreground. All four projects prove the OTP idioms that would let BATON-style burn-downs run unattended between human phase gates: one GenServer authority owns claims (no two processes can double-claim an item), a supervisor tree owns each run's lifecycle, and restart recovery costs nothing extra because the backlog file — already samen's source of truth — is re-read, not reconstructed from process memory. The honest scoping: building the full service is L; a thin mix samen.orch.tick implementing just poll→claim→dispatch against the existing backlog.yaml is M and captures most of the value.

Cluster 2 — durable run-lifecycle taxonomy, for ADR-047's in-product agent loop

A second cluster targets not samen's own dev process but the agent loop it ships inside products. ADR-047 already has budgets, cost caps, and transcript retention, but per the digest it has no liveness watchdog and no vocabulary richer than pass/fail for a run that stops partway. Four projects supply exactly that missing vocabulary, and they agree closely enough to read as one design: Symphony's run-attempt states (Succeeded | Failed | TimedOut | Stalled | CanceledByReconciliation) name two outcomes samen's RunRecord allowlist doesn't — stalled (no events within a timeout) and canceled-by-reconciliation (the underlying work item changed state out from under the run); Genesis's cancellation path distinguishes graceful (mark :cancelling, give the agent three full turns to wind down, auto-commit recovered state, terminal status :cancelled with a result) from forced (immediate kill, terminal status :failed with a nil result) — never letting a forced kill masquerade as a clean stop; Camelot's session-adoption reconciler solves the orthogonal problem of a run that finished correctly but the supervising process didn't survive to record it (tee output to a durable file, write an exit-code marker only after the tee completes, and on boot a pure recovery_action/4 decides adopt-vs-fail per orphaned session, re-attaching without re-running); Pixir's checkpoint_status vocabulary (checkpoint_ready | partial | failed | held | needs_orchestrator) adds the "partial-but-usable vs partial-but-blocking" distinction that samen's Automation engine's bounded-outcome RunRecord lacks today, with a dependent_safe flag gating whether downstream steps may consume a partial result. Read together, these close the exact gap the "resume re-runs only the stragglers" fan-out memory names: a durable-marker-plus-reconciler discipline lets a resumed session adopt completed-but-unharvested work rather than re-executing it.

Cluster 3 — cost governance and operator visibility

A third theme, less architecturally novel but directly fundable, is turning ADR-047's existing budgets/cost caps from a blunt cutoff into a governed, visible economic layer. Svärm's hard-vs-hold budget modes are the sharpest single idea: instead of a silent refusal when a cap is hit, hold parks the run and routes it through the same E3 approvals engine samen already has (requester ≠ approver, enforced in policy and DB-CHECK) — composing two existing subsystems instead of building a third. ControlKeel adds the layer above cutoffs: rolling 24-hour budget windows (not just per-run totals), preflight estimate-and-block, circuit breakers on burn rate and consecutive-failure rate (not just absolute spend), cached-token accounting, and allowlisted capture of provider rate-limit headers (anthropic-ratelimit-*, retry-after) — all describable, honestly, as "records telemetry, does not pretend to throttle," the same fail-honest framing samen already applies to its adapters. Genesis's per-model concurrency slot pools (capacity from config profiles, agents block on saturation, a PeakHourEngine that swaps capacity by IANA-timezone window) is the fleet-scale sibling of the same governance problem. On the visibility side, Svärm posts append-only cost receipts to each run (tokens, model, estimated USD, explicitly labeled "estimated") and a merge-rate/$-per-outcome ROI dashboard; Hive's Inference gateway meters billable usage per model binding at its relay chokepoint; both are read-model work over data samen's chokepoint already has — they feed the roadmap's open usage-rating gap with real numbers rather than requiring a new instrumentation point. Hive's "Flights" — durable, inspectable, resumable agent executions launched from a domain object, listed and filtered per-user — is the cleanest reference for the operator-plane surface all of this cost and state data should render into: a fleet-cockpit view of agent runs, not a raw log tail.

Cluster 1 — trust-plane and credential hygiene, from Codex Pooler and CYFR

Cluster 2 — agent-loop runtime hardening, from Neoharness

Neoharness ("my own take on openclaw") is a different animal from the other two: a single-user, single-node personal-agent runtime (Elixir 1.19+/OTP 27+, Phoenix LiveView, Postgres, Oban) with no policy layer, no masking, and direct AI writes to memory — the opposite of samen's trust posture on almost every axis. But its Agent.Loop.run/2 GenServer has clearly absorbed real production pain running an LLM tool-call loop unattended, and five of its hardening tripwires are cheap, clean-room-portable upgrades to ADR-047 regardless of the rest of the architecture. A duplicate tool-call guard tracks consecutive identical {name, arguments} pairs: a second repeat forces extended thinking, a third-plus blocks with a synthetic tool result (tools flagged allow_repeat: true for legitimate polling bypass the guard) — a cheaper, earlier tripwire against degenerate model loops than waiting for a budget cap to fire, and it keeps the transcript honest rather than silently truncating it. A three-level context-overflow recovery path handles the same failure at three different points: pre-turn trimming against a token budget, a mid-turn fold-and-retry-once when the provider rejects a too-long prompt, and post-turn extraction of durable facts before compaction — worth adopting with one caveat samen must enforce that Neoharness doesn't: any summarizer output is governed egress (EG2) and the fold has to run through the masking chokepoint with a per-turn grant re-scrub, and compacted history must stay crypto-shreddable (tokens, not plaintext) to respect the ADR-046 erasure envelope. A cancellation registry — ETS-backed cancel flags keyed by run id with a TTL, checked between loop iterations and before each tool call — gives the operator plane a real "stop this agent run" button with an auditable, non-lossy transcript tail, which is a named gap in samen's current operator-cockpit list. A circuit breaker on AI provider egress (Req transient retries plus a fuse that melts after N failures in a window and backs off the whole fleet for a cooldown, returning an honest {:error, :provider_unavailable} rather than a retry storm) is a direct extension of the breaker samen's automation engine already runs (ADR-039 Health/Breaker), just applied to the AI provider adapter layer instead — breaker state stays in the adapter package, never core, per INV-4. Two smaller items are worth a line each: isolated conversation ids for scheduled/cron agent runs (<agent>:cron:<name>) so heartbeat reasoning never pollutes a user-facing context budget, directly relevant once ADR-039 automation and ADR-047's loop wire together; and validate-then-retry-once-with-errors for structured LLM output against Ecto schemas, a cheap robustness pattern for samen's structured AI verbs (adopt the shape, not InstructorLite — keep the AI kernel vendor-free).

Cluster 2 — long-horizon memory: consolidation, chunking, and compaction under a context budget

A durable multi-turn agent loop eventually runs into two related but distinct pressures — the working context window fills up, and the useful facts from old sessions need to survive into new ones — and this scan turns up a clean division of labor between them. For the context-window pressure, OSA's 6-step pipeline (micro-compact old tool results, strip/merge tool args, structured compression, 413-recovery collapse, post-compact reinjection of critical context, priority-weighted budget allocation) and Pepe's simpler at-60%-replace-the-middle-with-a-summary approach — including Pepe's explicit tradeoff note that per-turn "micro-compaction" keeps cost steady but breaks provider prompt-cache reuse — are both directly portable to ADR-047's loop, with one non-negotiable samen twist neither source enforces: every compaction/summarization call is itself governed egress and must run through the masking chokepoint with a per-turn grant re-scrub, and the compacted history must stay crypto-shreddable (tokens, not restored plaintext) to respect the ADR-046 erasure envelope. For the cross-session pressure, Ankole's "Dreaming" is the standout design doc: a two-stage offline job (Stage A collapses raw chat into episode summaries; Stage B curates episodes into knowledge entries) triggered by silence/backlog thresholds, where the cursor advances only on success and every persisted block must cite a canonical source marker — paired with a before/after audit log that supports inverse-operation recovery (replay newest-to-oldest, stop on conflict rather than clobber) and withdrawal propagation (deleting a source reverses only the derived blocks that still match it). AlexClaw contributes the mechanical half that composes underneath either design: async embed-after-insert so writes never block on the embedding call, parent/child semantic chunking (children embedded, parent keeps full text, search dedups by parent), and embedding-model staleness tracking with a batch reembed_all. Ankole's recall recipe layers on top: hybrid keyword+vector via Reciprocal Rank Fusion, exponential temporal decay with a knowledge floor, and per-candidate token caps inside a global budget so one long thread can't starve others — directly upgradeable onto samen's existing pgvector + non-PII tsvector search using Postgres FTS instead of Ankole's ParadeDB dependency, so zero new infra. The unifying samen-specific constraint across all of it: consolidation, chunking, and recall all operate on already-masked content and gate any shared-store write through the E3 approvals engine — Ankole explicitly ships no approval flow for memory writes, which is the one place samen should deliberately diverge. Effort M for compaction (both sources), L for full Dreaming-style consolidation, S each for staleness tracking and parent/child chunking.

Cluster 3 — skills as data: progressive disclosure over hand-registered tools

Three projects converge on the same fix for a tool catalog that grows without bound: stop putting every tool/skill's full definition in every prompt, and instead give the model an index it can pull from on demand. ZAQ's "Open Agent Skills" implementation is the most rigorous: skills live as DB records (name, description, markdown body, tool keys); the system prompt carries only the name+description index (O(skill count) tokens, not O(skill count × body size)); a load_skill tool call pulls the full body as a tool result, scoped to the invoking agent's own enabled skills, with an explicit fix over the pattern's upstream form — a not-found response never enumerates the catalog, closing an info-leak. Long's version is filesystem-native (SKILL.md files with YAML frontmatter, a watcher-driven ETS index, per-member-vs-promoted-to-global scoping) and is the more portable authoring format since it's Anthropic-ecosystem-compatible — operators could author skills as files that mix samen.gen.agent then consumes. Vibe adds the generative end of the same loop: distilling a successful session into a reusable, versioned skill discovered from well-known paths ("create a skill from what just worked"). All three map onto the same samen shape — a skills-as-data extension of the Prompt resource, index-only in the system prompt, full body loaded via a governed tool call that stays inside the EG2 masking chokepoint, personal-vs-global scoping mapping to tenant-vs-operator plane, and any session-to-skill distillation routed through the normal review/approval path rather than auto-trusted. This is the most direct available design input for G22 (packaging the agent-grounding differentiator for builders), a gap the report currently has no other section closing. Related but adjacent, three projects add self-scheduling verbs worth folding into the same automation surface: Ankole's typed check_back_later wake event (a durable "check back at T" ActorEvent the loop consumes), Long's agent-initiated createScheduledTask mid-conversation, and Glorbo's heartbeat-with-empty-inbox grooming tick (agent dispatches against a checklist even when nothing is queued) — all three are thin wrappers over machinery samen already has (Oban + ash_oban + ADR-039's automation engine), landing as agent-proposable drafts routed through the E3 approvals engine, never auto-executed. Effort M for the skills-as-data extension, S–M for each self-scheduling verb.

Cluster 4 — the governance fine print: injection, attribution, identity, and honest replay

The remaining patterns are smaller but sharper, each closing one specific hole in the ADR-047 loop rather than a whole subsystem. AlexClaw's ContentSanitizer is the complementary threat to samen's egress-only chokepoint: a layered scrubber (zero-width Unicode stripping, hidden-HTML/CSS detection, a runtime-data injection-pattern list, an imperative-tone heuristic) applied to external content before it enters prompt assembly — samen governs what goes out via %MaskedPayload{}, but nothing today governs what comes in from tool results and web content, which is exactly what the ≥90% red-team eval bar should be measuring on both sides. Glorbo's per-dispatch egress attribution token (an ephemeral id minted per agent dispatch, stamped into every audit event downstream) and ZAQ's trusted-execution-context identity rule (identity for permission-scoped tools comes only from the loop's actor context, never from LLM-supplied parameters — an explicit skip_permissions: true opt-in required to override, and a blank/nil actor is never an implicit grant) are two sides of the same "know who really caused this" problem, and both are cheap: threading a dispatch id through the AI chokepoint into the hash-chained audit is a schema addition, and the actor-never-from-params rule is exactly the kind of AST/structural check samen's samen.verify.agent_coverage tier already does for other invariants. Shem contributes the most differentiated idea in the section: deterministic replay of recorded agent-loop turns as a CI regression tier (samen.verify.agent_replay-shaped — replay recorded events, diff against current code, fail on divergence) plus fail-closed declared-action tool manifests (undeclared action ⇒ refused, checked by a manifest-parity verifier) and offline-verifiable attestation bundles (a stdlib-only verifier script an auditor can run with no samen checkout) — all three extend samen's existing "every guarantee ships with a proof" culture into the AI plane specifically, and the replay tier is buildable now since EG2-governed transcripts and fixture-transport cassettes already exist. ZAQ's smaller persist-before-deliver ordering rule (transcript persistence must succeed before channel delivery, never delivered-but-unrecorded) is almost certainly already true of samen's delivery chokepoint but is worth encoding as a named, sabotage-able red-path test rather than left implicit. Vibe closes the loop with pure operator-plane ergonomics: first-class inspect/cancel/resume verbs on a running agent run (an operator "stop this run now" button, audited, mostly wiring existing OTP + AshStateMachine state to UI) and multi-surface attach (TUI and LiveView as adapters over one supervised session) as a live-attach affordance for the operator cockpit. Effort S–M across the cluster; the replay tier is the only M-leaning item, and none require new infrastructure beyond what ADR-046/047 already ships.

Deep Dive: Data Pipelines, Realtime & Observability/Ops

Five self-hosted Elixir/Phoenix projects, three different problem domains, one recurring lesson: mature BEAM systems that face CDC, realtime fan-out, or logging/alerting at production scale keep re-deriving the same handful of shapes — a stable idempotency key on every emitted event, a pluggable sink registry instead of a hard-coded egress path, Postgres as the only piece of infrastructure a cluster needs to find itself, and partition-drop instead of row-delete for high-churn retention tables. None of the five is a dependency samen should take: Sequin is CDC-focused and now maintenance-mode after an acquisition, Supabase Realtime and Logflare are both hosted-product codebases with no masking chokepoint, Plural Console is AGPL and solves Kubernetes fleet management (not SaaS tenancy), and ServiceRadar is a polyglot network-monitoring platform whose Elixir slice is one satellite among Go and Rust services. What all five earn is architecture reading against samen's own open gaps — multi-node Oban clustering, the single-sink wide-event exporter, retention-by-partition, and the still-open G11 alerting/status-page item — and in three cases (Sequin, Supabase Realtime, Logflare) the transferable pattern is concrete enough to scope at S–M effort without touching INV-4's vendor-free-core rule.

Cluster 1 — CDC and data-pipeline discipline, from Sequin

Sequin (sequinstream/sequin, MIT, Elixir/Phoenix/OTP, ~50k ops/s claimed) is a self-hosted Postgres change-data-capture platform: it attaches to a logical replication slot on any Postgres 14+ and streams row changes to 16+ sinks with strict per-key ordering. The company was acquired and Sequin Cloud shut down in late 2025, so the open-source repo is now explicitly maintenance-mode — fine as a reference codebase, wrong as a runtime dependency, and INV-4 would rule it out of core either way. Three patterns are worth re-implementing clean-room. The watermark-coordinated backfill — low/high watermark markers emitted around a chunked table scan, PK-filtered against changes seen between the marks, cursor persisted for crash-resume — is the correct, memory-bounded way to replay historical rows into a derived store on plain Postgres, directly relevant once samen's deferred ClickHouse rollup wiring (an open operator TODO) activates, and it composes cleanly with the ADR-015 default-deny CDC classifier since column filtering happens before egress either way. A stable idempotency key on every emitted event (slot LSN + PK, or backfill cursor + PK) is the cheapest win in the cluster: samen's webhook egress, Samen.Delivery.Chokepoint, notification fan-out, and fleet report wire already retry via Oban, and a first-party, verifier-checkable idempotency-key convention upgrades "at-least-once and hope" into auditable exactly-once semantics — a guarantee samen can sabotage-test. And Sequin's Broadway/GenStage sink pipelines, partitioned by primary key so per-row ordering survives parallelization, is the canonical OTP answer for the day samen's fan-out (webhooks, ESP delivery, fleet reports, tsvector maintenance at scale) needs more throughput than discrete Oban jobs give it — read Sequin's SlotProcessor before designing that subsystem, and land Broadway itself in a samen_web/adapter package only, never core. The one item to explicitly reject is Sequin's security model: it streams raw row data to external sinks by design, no masking, no vault awareness — the precise anti-pattern samen's hero claim exists to prevent, so any CDC egress in samen stays behind the classifier and the token-blind wire regardless of what pattern is borrowed above it.

Cluster 2 — realtime, clustering, and retention, from Supabase Realtime

Supabase Realtime (supabase/realtime, Apache-2.0, ~7.6k stars, actively developed) is the WebSocket server behind Supabase's Postgres Changes/Broadcast/Presence features — a globally distributed Elixir cluster, multi-tenant by design, explicitly at-most-once ("does not guarantee that every message will be delivered"). The product itself is not adoptable: Broadcast payloads go to clients as raw JSON with no masking chokepoint, and samen's chat/notifications/operator cockpit are already served in-process by LiveView and Phoenix.PubSub, so bolting on a separate WebSocket server would only add an ungoverned egress surface. But three infrastructure choices inside it are the section's best single steal. libcluster + libcluster_postgres discovers cluster nodes through the Postgres database itself — no epmd, no DNS, no Kubernetes — which closes samen's own listed gap ("Oban multi-node in production: proven locally only, via a two-BEAM-node :peer sim") with zero new infrastructure, exactly the "encode knowledge as infra" and Postgres-only-purity posture samen already holds; it's a small hex dep at the host/deploy layer, not core, so INV-4 doesn't even need to weigh in. Partition-by-day with drop-partition retention — Realtime's realtime.messages table is daily-partitioned with a 3-day retention enforced by dropping old partitions rather than deleting rows — is an O(1), vacuum-free pattern that maps directly onto samen's own retention subsystem, wide-event/audit/notification streams, and the ADR-046 transcript-retention rule, and composes cleanly with the crypto-shred story since tokens-only rows simply expire by partition. And per-tenant rate/usage counters (ETS/counters flushed periodically to telemetry, feeding both throttling and metrics) generalize samen's current Hammer-backed ingress-only rate limiting into something that can back ADR-047 AI budget enforcement, the automation engine's existing Health/Breaker, and eventual usage-based billing. One more idea is worth naming without adopting the mechanism: Realtime authorizes channel joins by asking the tenant's own Postgres (RLS on realtime.messages) — the samen-native equivalent, if tenant-facing realtime subscriptions ever ship, is routing channel-join through Ash policies and OrgScope at a single subscribe chokepoint, same seam, samen's policy engine instead of RLS.

Cluster 3 — sink registries and alert loops, from Logflare, Plural Console, and ServiceRadar

The remaining three projects converge on the same lesson from different angles: once an event or metric leaves the code that produced it, route it through one small, uniform behaviour instead of hard-coding the destination — and Sequin's Broadway sink delivery (Cluster 1) is really the same idea from the ingestion side, so the pattern shows up on both ends of samen's pipeline. Logflare (Logflare/logflare, Apache-2.0, Supabase-owned production SaaS) structures multi-backend fan-out as an Adaptor behaviour — cast_config/validate_config/transform_config per backend type, a type→module registry, one supervised process pair per {source, backend} — which is the clean, minimal-surface way to grow samen's currently single-sink wide-event exporter (samen_core/lib/samen/wide_event/sinks/otlp.ex) into a plural story (webhook/Slack for operator alerts, a Postgres sink for local dev) without touching the emit path; effort S–M. Layered on top of that same adaptor set, Logflare's AlertQuery loop — a stored query, a cron schedule via Oban, a threshold check, dispatch through the adaptors — is a direct design blueprint for samen's still-open G11 "public status/SLA + alerting" gap, mapping onto samen's own Samen.Metrics.definitions/0 and Prometheus egress; samen would harden it further with fail-honest behavior on an unconfigured notify target and approvals-gating if an alert can trigger a mutating action. Logflare's third contribution is narrower but concrete: its Cloak.Vault key ring (default/retired/fallback keys plus a do_migrate/0 re-encryption path) is a well-tested rotation state machine worth adapting for Samen.Kms's operator-facing key-rotation UX — samen keeps its own AES-256-GCM vault (ADR-003 already rejected Cloak itself for the PII path), only the rotation shape transfers. Plural Console (pluralsh/console, AGPL-3.0 — patterns only, no code lift) is a Kubernetes-fleet control plane whose domain doesn't transfer, but three of its ops-library choices do: prom_ex auto-instruments Phoenix/Ecto/Oban/BEAM metrics on top of the same :telemetry events samen already emits, additive and cheap (verify it doesn't leak pii_-prefixed labels via the existing samen.verify.metric_labels tier); its per-rule multi-channel notification fan-out (one event, routed by rule to Slack, Teams, and email concurrently) is the shape for a future samen_slack/samen_teams adapter pair once the fleet cockpit's notifications module needs more than inbox+prefs; and its pull-based, reverse-tunnel agent model — managed nodes poll the control plane and tunnel back, never receiving inbound connections — is a naming/stance clarification samen's ADR-044 fleet cockpit already leans toward but hasn't committed to explicitly, worth locking in as directives execution matures past observability. ServiceRadar (Carver Automation, Apache-2.0) is the outlier: a network-monitoring platform with an Ash/Phoenix/Oban control plane wrapped by Go and Rust satellites for collection and query execution. Nothing in its product surface overlaps samen's mission, and it has no PII vaulting, masking, or crypto-shred equivalent to compare against — but it stands as real-world validation that Ash/Phoenix/Oban can serve as the "brain" of a production system while performance-sensitive edges live in satellite services, reinforcing rather than contradicting samen's ADR-037 lean-kernel posture. Its Rust-NIF-embedded query engine (Rustler, not a sidecar) is worth a line as a precedent if samen ever needs a compiled expression evaluator inside samen_web, but nothing on the roadmap calls for it today.

Cluster 2 — DNS-rebinding-proof outbound fetch, two implementations of the same three layers

Samen's fail-honest adapters and Samen.Delivery.Chokepoint already take operator- or tenant-supplied URLs (webhook subscriptions, org verification, fleet heartbeat endpoints per ADR-044), and the digest doesn't record a rebinding-proof answer for any of them — Baudrate and vutuv both do, and they land on the identical three-layer shape independently. Layer one is a literal, no-DNS check safe to run inside a changeset — reject localhost/private/link-local/reserved ranges outright, and vutuv is explicit that this must also catch the cloud metadata IP (169.254.169.254). Layer two closes the gap layer one cannot: resolve the hostname's A/AAAA records at fetch time, because a public hostname can have a DNS record that resolves to an internal address — this is the actual SSRF defense, not the literal check. Layer three closes the TOCTOU window between layer two's lookup and the real connection: pin the outbound connection to the exact IP just validated and set the Host header manually, so there is no second DNS lookup for an attacker to race or poison. Baudrate names this "ADR-0007" and applies it to every outbound POST including Web Push; vutuv's version (Vutuv.Ssrf) goes one step further, using the vetted-address pin to drive a headless-browser screenshot fetch through a loopback SOCKS5 proxy that dials only that address, covering redirects and in-page navigation too. Both make the resolver injectable for deterministic tests. This is a clean fit for samen's existing chokepoint discipline (`Samen.Delivery.Chokepoint`, the AI egress chokepoint) — one client, enforced by a verifier that no module calls `Req` directly outside it, exactly the "one place to audit" shape samen already applies elsewhere. Baudrate additionally specs a bounded hostile-fetch walk (≤N hops, ≤M distinct hosts, visited-URI cycle guard, rate limits keyed on the target host as well as the sender) for the day samen agents or link-unfurl features fetch attacker-influenced URLs.
